Histology - System Interpretation

PTC - Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma

Lowest baseline aggressiveness. Risk driven by extent, nodal burden, and ETE. RAI avidity usually preserved. Vascular invasion is a weaker prognostic driver than in follicular-pattern tumours.

IEFVPTC - Infiltrative/Encapsulated FVPTC

Encapsulated disease is indolent; infiltrative disease carries higher recurrence risk. RAI generally preserved but can rise with infiltration or significant vascular invasion.

FTC - Follicular Thyroid Carcinoma

Vascular invasion burden is the dominant prognostic driver. Haematogenous spread more common; nodal disease uncommon. RAI variable. <4 foci = lower/intermediate; ≥4 foci = high risk.

OTC - Oncocytic (Hurthle cell)

Higher baseline aggressiveness than FTC. RAI avidity lower and less predictable. Any vascular invasion escalates risk; ≥4 foci treated as high-risk biology.

Unfavourable Histology Subtypes - ATA 2025

The ATA 2025 guidelines recognise several histological subtypes of DTC that carry intrinsically higher recurrence risk and/or poorer prognosis than classical PTC. Selecting one of these automatically escalates the ATA recurrence risk category.

Tall Cell Variant PTC (>30% tall cells)

Tall cells defined as height ≥2x width. BRAF V600E highly prevalent (~80%). Carries higher recurrence and disease-specific mortality vs classical PTC. Cannot be classified as Low Risk. Associated with reduced RAI avidity and BRAF-mediated dedifferentiation.

Columnar Cell Variant PTC

Rare, aggressive variant. Nuclear stratification is the hallmark. High recurrence and mortality rates. Total thyroidectomy and adjuvant RAI strongly recommended. Classify as High Risk.

Hobnail / Micropapillary Variant PTC

≥30% hobnail features (loss of cellular polarity, prominent nucleoli, micropapillary architecture). BRAF V600E common. Associated with higher recurrence and disease-related mortality. Classify as at least Intermediate-High.

Diffuse Sclerosing Variant PTC

Bilateral diffuse thyroid involvement, dense fibrosis, psammoma bodies, squamous metaplasia, lymphocytic infiltration, frequent lymph node metastases. At least Intermediate risk; total thyroidectomy required.

Solid / Trabecular Variant (>30%)

>30% solid/trabecular growth in otherwise DTC morphology. Must be distinguished from PDTC (apply Turin criteria). Associated with higher recurrence. At least Intermediate risk.

Poorly Differentiated Thyroid Carcinoma (PDTC)

Defined by Turin criteria: solid/trabecular/insular growth PLUS at least one of: convoluted nuclei, mitoses ≥3/10 HPF, or coagulative tumour necrosis. Limited RAI responsiveness; early systemic therapy consideration. High Risk by definition.

OTC with High-Grade Features

Oncocytic carcinoma with mitoses ≥3/10 HPF or tumour necrosis. Classified separately from standard OTC under WHO 2022 classification. Poor RAI avidity; High Risk. Systemic therapy consideration early.

Multifocality - ATA 2025 Context

Multifocal DTC (two or more distinct tumour foci) is common in PTC. ATA 2025 provides the following guidance:

Unifocal disease

Standard risk stratification applies based on size, extent, nodes, and margins alone.

Multifocal - ipsilateral (same lobe), 2 foci

Ipsilateral multifocality alone is generally not a significant independent risk escalator for intrathyroidal disease. Risk is driven by the dominant (largest) focus and aggregate adverse features.

Multifocal - bilateral or ≥3 foci

Bilateral multifocality and/or ≥3 foci may upgrade recurrence risk, particularly when combined with other intermediate- or high-risk features (e.g. ETE, large nodes, unfavourable histology). This system applies a risk increment for bilateral or extensively multifocal disease.

Surgical implication

Bilateral multifocal PTC is one of the strongest indications for total thyroidectomy over hemithyroidectomy. It ensures complete excision, enables Tg surveillance, and facilitates RAI if required. Ipsilateral multifocality of ≤2 foci does not automatically require total thyroidectomy if other features support lobectomy.

RAI - Intent-Based Guide (ATA 2025)

ATA 2025 guidelines support intent-based selection of RAI activity rather than fixed dosing. Individualise based on intent, pathological risk features, disease burden, and MDT nuclear medicine input.

Remnant Ablation (~30 mCi / 1.1 GBq)

Elimination of normal thyroid remnant post-surgery. For low-risk and selected intermediate-risk patients. Comparable efficacy to higher activities with reduced toxicity.

Adjuvant Therapy (~75-150 mCi / 2.8-5.5 GBq)

Reduction of recurrence risk with adverse pathological features. Context-dependent; integrated risk assessment not histology alone.

Treatment of Known Iodine-Avid Disease

Higher activities; empiric or dosimetry-guided. Especially for distant metastases or repeat treatment. Nuclear medicine-led; diagnostic WBS recommended first.

About ThyroidCA

ThyroidCA is a browser-based clinical decision support tool for the multidisciplinary management of differentiated thyroid carcinoma (DTC), developed and maintained by Professor Steven C. Boyages at the EndoHormone Institute (EHI), Sydney. The tool synthesises the 2025 American Thyroid Association (ATA) guidelines to provide structured, individually tailored management recommendations spanning surgical planning, radioactive iodine therapy, TSH suppression, systemic and targeted therapy, serum Tg interpretation, and post-treatment surveillance.

The tool was developed to address a recognised gap in accessible, guideline-aligned decision support for DTC management in clinical practice. While the ATA 2025 guidelines are comprehensive, their application to individual patients — with their specific combinations of tumour, pathological, and molecular features — requires integration of multiple interacting variables. ThyroidCA operationalises this integration in real time, providing a structured aide-memoire for clinical encounters, MDT discussions, and post-operative planning.

The AI pathology auto-fill feature uses the Anthropic Claude API to extract structured clinical data directly from uploaded surgical pathology reports (PDF or image), reducing manual data entry and transcription error. Documents are processed via an encrypted server-side proxy; no patient data is stored by EHI.

How the Risk Engine Works

ThyroidCA implements a weighted scoring algorithm aligned to the ATA 2025 recurrence risk stratification framework. Each clinical and pathological feature is assigned a score based on its established prognostic weight in the guidelines:

High-weight features (score 25–55): Distant metastasis (M1), gross extrathyroidal extension, R2 resection, unfavourable histology (PDTC, columnar cell, OTC high-grade), extensive vascular invasion, high-volume nodal disease, extranodal extension (ENE)
Intermediate-weight features (score 10–25): R1 resection, unfavourable histology (tall cell, hobnail, diffuse sclerosing, solid/trabecular), minimal vascular invasion, low-volume nodal disease, tumour size 2–4 cm, bilateral/extensive multifocality, non-intrathyroidal disease
Lower-weight features (score 4–10): Age ≥55 years, ipsilateral multifocality, nodal micrometastases, rising post-operative Tg, tumour size >4 cm

Risk tiers: Low (score <12), Intermediate (12–34), High (≥35). Certain features (M1 disease, PDTC, columnar cell carcinoma) trigger automatic High-risk classification regardless of score. The surgical procedure type modifies downstream recommendations — particularly RAI eligibility and Tg surveillance — without changing the tumour risk tier itself.

Intended Users

ThyroidCA is designed for use by qualified healthcare professionals involved in the management of thyroid cancer, including endocrinologists, endocrine surgeons, nuclear medicine physicians, medical oncologists, radiation oncologists, and clinical nurse specialists. It is intended as a structured aide for clinical decision-making, MDT preparation, and post-operative planning — not as a standalone diagnostic or prescriptive tool.

Optimal use involves entering pathological data from the surgical pathology report, adding available molecular and biochemical data, and reviewing the generated plan in the context of the individual patient's clinical circumstances, comorbidities, and preferences. The AI auto-fill feature can accelerate data entry from pathology reports but should always be reviewed for accuracy before a plan is generated.

Limitations

ThyroidCA provides structured guideline-based recommendations and cannot account for all factors relevant to individual clinical decisions. Known limitations include:

⚠ Clinical factors not captured: Full comorbidity burden, functional status, patient values and preferences, shared decision-making nuance, institutional expertise and resource availability, contraindications to specific therapies beyond those explicitly entered, drug interactions, and the evolving clinical trajectory over time.

⚠ Guideline currency: Recommendations are aligned to ATA 2025 as interpreted at the time of development. Thyroid cancer guidelines are periodically updated; users should verify currency against the most recent published ATA guidance and emerging evidence.

⚠ AI extraction accuracy: The pathology auto-fill feature relies on Claude's interpretation of unstructured text. Extracted fields should always be verified against the original pathology report before a plan is generated. Accuracy may be lower for scanned or low-quality documents.

⚠ Scope: ThyroidCA covers DTC only (PTC, FTC, OTC, and variants). It does not cover anaplastic thyroid carcinoma, medullary thyroid carcinoma, thyroid lymphoma, or metastatic disease to the thyroid.

Future Development

Planned features under active development at EHI include: integration of ATA response-to-therapy reassessment categories (Excellent / Indeterminate / Biochemical Incomplete / Structural Incomplete) with dynamic follow-up plan generation; TNM staging calculator (AJCC 8th edition); paediatric DTC module; anaplastic and medullary thyroid carcinoma decision support; export to structured PDF report; and integration with Australian PBS/TGA prescribing information for targeted therapies. Guideline updates will be incorporated as new ATA recommendations are published.
